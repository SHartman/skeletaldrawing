It's been two weeks and change since I launched a ground-up rewrite of the website, and the reviews have been almost entirely positive, and occasionally effusive. I'm delighted that the time I invested into the overhaul seems to have been well worth it, and particularly that others agree that mobile navigation is now much better than it used to be.

Since then I've been trying to add some of the rigorous skeletals I skipped because they took too much time to update before the launch, and made a few polish updates under the hood. But I did get one request I wanted to address, involving the loss of the last-modified dates I used to include on each image. When I did the redesign, I wanted to add my website alongside the copyright claim, so when users post images in forums or on social media people can see where to find the originals. To prevent the watermark from getting out of control, I sacrificed the "last modified" date.

I didn't think this was too big of a loss, as those dates were largely intended for my own use, so I can keep track of which version is the most recent. But it turns out several of you also made use of them, presumably also to track when I made updates. I don't want the new website to be a downgrade in any way, so I spent the last week and a half making a database of all of the last edited dates for the skeletals I've posted. And since I had to track down the metadata for each image in their respective folders, I went ahead and added the date they were first created as well.

So now every skeletal image should have its birth year, and the year it was most recently modified. I only included origin dates for images that are actually connected in some way to the current skeletal - for example in the pre-website era I had done a _Coelophysis,_ and a _Tyrannosaurus_ based on Sue (FMNH PR 2018) that were ultimately completely abandoned before starting again, so those dates reflect the newer starting-over point. As such, the oldest skeletal drawing on the site is _Psittacosaurus_, which I drew in my first-ever apartment, in Casper, Wyoming back in 1996 (funny story about that - I had been given a bag of chocolate-covered espresso beans, and in my youth didn't realize that eating them would be literally like eating coffee. Not accustomed to caffeine at that point, I found myself unable to sleep for almost 24 hours, and drew the psittacosaur skeletal out of boredom as I waited for the caffeine to wear off). Of course it's been heavily modified several times since then, but those have built upon the original (as scanned into the digital world around 1998), so I consider it "the same image."
