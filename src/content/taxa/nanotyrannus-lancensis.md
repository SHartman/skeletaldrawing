_Nanotyrannus lancensis_ was a medium-sized, gracile eutyrannosaur from the Late Cretaceous of North America. Based on NCSM 40000 (restored here), it appears _N. lancensis_ grew close to 6.5 m (21 feet) long. Still a big predator by modern standards, but quite a bit smaller than the large _Tyrannosaurus_ individuals it shared its environment with.

As you can read in more depth on the [genus hub page](https://www.skeletaldrawing.com/theropods/nanotyrannus/), _Nanotyrannus_ was originally based on a less-than-ideally preserved skull and jaws, and has been the subject of decades of debate over whether these smaller tyrannosaur specimens were juvenile _T. rex_, or whether a second, smaller predator lived alongside it.

That question was definitively answered with the Bloody Mary specimen (NCSM 40000), as an examination of tissue growth patterns in the femur (thigh bone) show it was an adult, despite being around half the length of a fully-grown _T. rex_. Other differences from _T. rex_ included the larger hands of Nanotyrannu_s_, and several features of the skull.
