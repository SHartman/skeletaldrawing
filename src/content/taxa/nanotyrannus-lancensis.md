_Nanotyrannus lancensis_ was a medium-sized, gracile eutyrannosaur from the Late Cretaceous of North America. Based on NCSM 40000 (restored here), it appears _N. lancensis_ grew close to 6.5 m (21 feet) long. Still a bit predator by modern standards, but quite a bit smaller than the large _Tyrannosaurus_ individuals it shared its environment with.

The life and times of the name _Nanotyrannus lancensis_ has been fraught with changed ideas and forks in the road. The first specimen, a somewhat batter skull, was originally named as a new species of _Gorgosaurus_. The new skull was slightly younger than other _Gorgosaurus_ remains (_Nanotyrannus_ is found in rocks from the very end of the Cretaceous, where we find _Tyrannosaurus rex_), but only by a couple of million years, nothing that would be particularly surprising.

In 1988 a group of researchers felt the skull (preserved in the Cleveland Museum of Natural History) was too different from _Gorgosaurus_ to remain in the same genus, so they gave it the new (and now current) name: _Nanotyrannus_. This was largely unchallenged for most of a decade, although with only a skull, there were lots of questions left unknown about _Nanotyrannus_. 

One question that vexed paleontologists was why we don't find juvenile _T. rex_ specimens, but did start finding specimens that were referred to _Nanotyrannus_? That seemed to be answered in the late 1990s, when some researchers reinterpretted all small tyrannosaur specimens from the latest Cretaceous as juvenile _T. rex_. This nicely answered the question of where the little rexes were, but not everyone agreed.

The topic remained contentious until the publication of NCSM 40000 - a specimen of gracile eutyrannosaur that was fully grown, at a size far smaller than that of _Tyrannosaurus_. It also shared some key skull features with the original skull in Cleveland, and so now we have a virtually complete _Nanotyrannus lancensis_. and there really was a smallish, fast eutyrannosaur living alongside its fearsome giant relative, _Tyrannosaurus rex._
