_Diplodocus_ was a lightly built and exceptionally long sauropod dinosaur; despite reaching lengths of 30+ meters (98+ feet), it was relatively low in mass, usually in the 10-15 tonne range. _Diplodocus_ dwelt in the famous Late Jurassic Morrison Formation of western North America. It lived alongside plant-eaters like _Stegosaurus_, _Brachiosaurus_ and _Apatosaurus_, while avoiding predation from theropods such as _Allosaurus_, _Ceratosaurus_ and _Torvosaurus_. 

_Diplodocus_ hasn't suffered as exciting a series of naming debates as _Apatosaurus_ or _Allosaurus_, but has run into a situation where the type species, _D. longus_ (known from a handful of tail vertebrae), is considered by some to be insufficiently diagnostic to be valid. Attempts to petition the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature to switch the type species to the more completely documented _D. carnegii_ were rejected, so we will have to watch where future naming issues go.

Another (and potentially related) issue involves the genus _Seismosaurus_. Originally described as a new type of diplodocid, often with fantastical size projections, more complete excavation and preparation of the specimen showed that it was in fact a species of _Diplodocus_. So _Seismosaurus hallorum_ became _Diplodocus hallorum_, and it has been shown that many specimens originally described as _D. longus_ group with it...

...which raises an interesting question. Rather than change the type species of _Diplodocus_, perhaps someone will either find the original _D. longus_ specimen to be diagnostic (in which case _D. hallorum_ might get swallowed up entirely into it), or another author could suggest using the obviously similar _D. hallorum_ as a neotype for _D. longus_. Only time will tell which of these outcomes, if either, comes to fruition.
