The name _Torvosaurus_ only dates back to 1979, but fossils that would be attributed to it have been found in Late Jurassic rocks for well over a century in the western US, Tanzania, and more recently western Europe. The name _Torvosaurus_ was given to fossils found in the famous Dry Mesa Quarry, a site in the Morrison Formation filled with large sauropod remains (the type specimen of _Supersaurus_ was also found here).

Unfortunately all of the material in Dry Mesa was disarticulated, so _Torvosaurus_ has come to light in bits and pieces. In the 1990s more isolated material was found in Como Bluff, Wyoming (originally given the names _Edmarka_ and _Brontoraptor_), which added more material, including highly robust hind-limb elements. 

In 2012, a single specimen that was 55% complete was discovered in Colorado. This specimen, nicknamed Elvis, is on display at the Museum of Natural History & Science in Cincinnati, although we are still waiting on a more complete description of the specimen (science and writing take time!).

Meanwhile, a second species was formally described from Portugal in 2014: _Torvosaurus gurneyi_ (fun fact, _T. gurneyi_ was named in honor of artist James Gurney, who created the Dinotopia books). _T. gurneyi_ is still pretty scrappy, but at least it has a name now - the African material still sits as isolated bones, sometimes referred to _Torvosaurus sp._, sometimes just as "indeterminate megalosaur."
